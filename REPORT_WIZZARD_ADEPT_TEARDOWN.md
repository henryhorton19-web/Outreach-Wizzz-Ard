# PART 2: AS A COLD OUTREACH PRACTITIONER

## 2.1 The opener hits two of the five named anti-patterns exactly

The published "do not use" list for 2026 openers includes, verbatim:

> <cite index="30-1">"Congrats on the recent funding round." Funding announcement scrapers have used this so
> heavily that prospects associate the line with cold outreach automatically.</cite>

and

> <cite index="30-1">"I noticed [company] is growing quickly." Means nothing. Every B2B company says they
> are growing. The line provides zero signal of real research.</cite>

*"Your recent acquisitions and inclusion in the FrenchTech 120 index point to a period of aggressive
expansion"* **is** the second one, dressed up. So is *"this seems like a pivotal moment for operational
scaling."* Both announce a growth trend, which every company is in, so both provide zero research signal
while occupying the highest-value line in the email.

The correct frame is stated plainly: <cite index="30-1">The strongest first lines in 2026 are situational,
not complimentary.</cite>

## 2.2 The penalty concentrates exactly where these emails are weakest

A regression over 100,000 AI-sent emails found the damage is not spread evenly:

> <cite index="19-1">The interesting pattern in the regression is that the penalties are concentrated in
> opener and vocabulary rather than in the substantive body of the email. AI gets the middle of the email
> roughly right; it leaks signal at the edges, the template-y opener, the cliché closer, the absent
> signature block.</cite>

These drafts leak at all three edges at once: a template-y opener, a closer repeated word for word across
both emails, and no signature block at all.

## 2.3 The cheapest available win is being skipped entirely

> <cite index="19-1">Missing signature structure (no name + title + LinkedIn link): when the signature
> includes all three, reply rate lifts +9%. Most AI prompts ship without signature templating; this is a
> one-line prompt fix worth more than most copy optimizations.</cite>

**+9% for one line of templating, and the drafts have none.** This is the highest return per unit of effort
available anywhere in this system.

## 2.4 The value-first structure is half-built

> <cite index="33-1">One practitioner who tracked 200+ replies found that switching from an ask-first
> opener to a value-first opener lifted reply rates from 0.8% to 3-4%. The mechanism is reciprocity, you
> are giving before asking, which earns the right to a response.</cite>

The close does this correctly: *"Happy to do a short piece of it first."* That is a genuine give-before-ask
and it should stay. But the body gives nothing, so the offer arrives with no evidence behind it, and
because the same sentence closes both emails it reads as a template even to a single reader.

## 2.5 The penalty is about perceived effort, not prose quality

The most useful framing in the research, because it explains why short-and-empty is worse than
long-and-specific:

> <cite index="20-1">56% preferred the AI-written article in the blind comparison. Yet 52% said they would
> become less engaged with copy they suspected was AI-generated. Same readers, opposite reactions. They
> liked the words. They disliked what the words implied about the sender. Because the penalty is about
> perceived effort, not sentence quality.</cite>

And the operational conclusion: <cite index="20-1">do not obsess over whether prospects can detect AI. Make
sure the final message demonstrates human judgment, because that is what they are actually testing you
for.</cite>

Neither email demonstrates judgement. Both demonstrate retrieval.

## 2.6 The system had the data and produced filler anyway

This is the specific failure the practitioner literature warns about:

> <cite index="21-1">Feed it real data, never let it guess. If the model does not have a concrete fact
> about the prospect, it will invent vague filler. No data means no personalized line.</cite>

**Here there was data.** Partoo's cache held six substantive facts and the email used one, as decoration.
Ouihelp's held five and the email used one, as decoration. The failure is not missing research, it is a
composition step that ignores the research it was handed.

The corollary is worth keeping: <cite index="30-1">A generic Direct Honest opener will out-perform a fake
Situational one.</cite> An honest "I do not know your business, here is what I do" beats a manufactured
observation.

## 2.7 The measured cost of getting this wrong

| Personalisation tier | Reply rate |
|---|---|
| Generic templates | <cite index="22-1">~1%</cite> |
| First name only | <cite index="22-1">~1.5% to 2%</cite> |
| Deep text personalisation | <cite index="22-1">~5% to 8%</cite> |

<cite index="22-1">The lift from generic to deeply personalized is not incremental; it is
exponential.</cite> These two emails sit in the first or second tier despite having the raw material for
the third.

On the fully-automated question, the sources genuinely disagree and both should be reported.
<cite index="24-1">Fully AI-generated emails, generated from a prompt with no human editing, typically get
reply rates 30 to 50% lower than human-written</cite>, while another 2026 dataset puts it at
<cite index="26-1">roughly a 1.1 percentage-point reply-rate gap between fully AI-generated sends and
human-written ones on comparable lists, a meaningful improvement from a ~2 percentage-point gap measured in
2024</cite>. The honest reading is that the gap is real, narrowing, and depends heavily on whether the
output looks generic. <cite index="27-1">Generic AI email gets flagged. Considered AI-assisted email does
not.</cite>

## 2.8 One thing already correct

Em-dash density is a documented tell: <cite index="19-1">more than 2 em-dashes per email, minus 8% reply
rate; em-dash density is a strong AI signal, humans use 0 to 1 em-dashes per cold email.</cite> The voice
already sets `allow_dashes: false` and the drafts contain none. Keep that.

# PART 3: CODEBASE DIAGNOSIS

## 3.1 The identical proposal is a scoring bug, and it is my error from the previous turn

`engine/draft_engine.py:203`:

```python
standing = C.CANDIDATE_PROFILE.get("standing_key", "anchor_co")
if key == standing:
    score += 1
```

`standing_key` adds a flat `+1` to one experience **on every company**. I recommended setting it to
`outreach_system`, and `wizzard_adept.json` sets `evidence.count: 1`. Reproduced:

```
Partoo  (local marketing SaaS):   2  outreach_system   -> count=1 picks: outreach_system
Ouihelp (elderly care):           2  outreach_system   -> count=1 picks: outreach_system
any company at all:               2  outreach_system   -> count=1 picks: outreach_system
```

**Every company gets the same proposal, by construction.** Compounding it, `_target_bridge_tags` returns
`["analytical", "builds"]` for essentially every company, so the tag intersection cannot discriminate
either, which was diagnosed earlier in this project and never fixed.

## 3.2 The noticing block has no mechanism forcing a number

Its guidance says to include a verbatim specific, and nothing checks. When the observation is weak the
model falls back to a trend statement, which is the anti-pattern in 2.1. The `draft_shape.py` module built
in a prior plan does check for an unused specific, but it accepts any distinctive **word**, so *"7,500
clients"* appearing in a subordinate clause satisfies it.

## 3.3 The close is fixed text and therefore identical everywhere

Correct as a design choice, and it means roughly 40% of a 65-word email is boilerplate shared across every
send. That is fine at low volume and a template signal at scale.

## 3.4 The token economics are broken

| Company | Input | Output | Cost | Words shipped |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Partoo | 7,306 | 9,466 | $0.0271 | ~65 |
| Ouihelp | 7,433 | 12,793 | $0.0354 | ~65 |

A 65-word email is roughly 85 tokens. **Over 99% of generated output is discarded**, at 146 and 197 output
tokens per word that shipped. The likely cause is the stacked verbalized-sampling and revision pipeline
from earlier plans, each stage generating full candidate sets. A 500-company run costs about $15 to produce
two-paragraph emails, which is not fatal but is a signal that the pipeline is doing far more generation
than the output justifies.

# PART 4: WHAT THE EVIDENCE SAYS TO DO

Ranked by measured return, highest first.

| Fix | Basis | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| Add a signature block: name, one-line identity, link | +9% reply rate, one line of templating | Trivial |
| Kill the growth-trend opener, require a number in the first sentence | The two named anti-patterns, and penalties concentrate at the opener | Small |
| Unpin `standing_key`, raise `evidence.count` to 2 | Reproduced scoring bug producing identical proposals | Small |
| Make the proposal about **their** business, not the sender's last project | The founder read, both emails | Medium |
| Honest fallback when there is no angle, rather than a manufactured one | A generic honest opener beats a fake situational one | Small |
| Reduce sampling waste | 99% of output discarded | Medium |

**The through-line:** every one of these makes the email demonstrate that someone looked, which is what the
recipient is actually testing for. The penalty is about perceived effort, and effort is legible only
through specificity.
